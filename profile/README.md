# Applied Modeling & Infrastructure Analytics

**Python | Probability & Statistics | Monte Carlo Simulation | Predictive Analytics | Engineering Decision Support**

An independent collection of applied modeling, analytical software, and computational research projects focused on infrastructure engineering, uncertainty, risk, and capital investment decisions.

This GitHub organization serves as the central home for my analytics projects, bringing together engineering domain knowledge, statistical methods, simulation, and software development.

The objective is to explore how quantitative methods can improve engineering decisions, particularly when dealing with incomplete information, uncertain future conditions, constrained budgets, and complex infrastructure systems.

Applying statistical methods to characterize variability, evaluate assumptions, quantify uncertainty, and support defensible engineering conclusions.

Methods of interest include:

- Descriptive and inferential statistics
- Probability distributions
- Monte Carlo simulation
- Bayesian inference and MCMC

Using engineering data and computational methods to investigate infrastructure behavior, performance, reliability, and operational risk.

Applications include:

- Water and wastewater infrastructure

Areas of interest include:

- Probabilistic capital cost estimating
- Capital improvement planning

- Risk-informed project prioritization
- Lifecycle cost evaluation
- Budget constraints and investment tradeoffs
- Scenario-based decision support

### Analytical Software Development

Building reproducible analytical workflows and reusable Python-based tools that translate engineering data and mathematical models into interpretable results.

Emphasis is placed on:

- Data ingestion and validation
- Exploratory data analysis
- Modular analytical code
- Simulation and numerical methods
- Automated testing

- Visualization and reporting

## Modeling Philosophy

The purpose of applied modeling is not simply to produce numerical results.

A useful model should help answer an engineering or business question, communicate uncertainty, and support a decision.

Projects within this organization are guided by several principles:

**Engineering context matters.** Analytical methods should reflect the physical, operational, and financial conditions of the system being studied.

**Uncertainty should be quantified.** Single-point estimates often conceal important risks and assumptions.

**Model complexity should be justified.** More sophisticated methods are not necessarily more useful. The appropriate approach depends on the available evidence and the decision being evaluated.

**Reproducibility is essential.** Analytical workflows should be documented, testable, and repeatable.

**Results require interpretation.** Statistical significance, predictive performance, and simulated outcomes do not automatically establish engineering significance.

## Project Development & Versioning

Projects are developed incrementally, with an emphasis on establishing clearly defined analytical objectives and release milestones.

Where appropriate, repositories use Semantic Versioning:

`MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`

Project lifecycle stages include:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Planning | Scope, methodology, and data requirements are being defined. |
| Active Development | Core analytical or software functionality is being implemented. |
| Stable Release | The defined initial scope has been completed and validated. |
| Maintenance & Enhancements | The project has reached a stable release and continues to receive improvements. |
| Archived | Development has concluded or the repository is retained for reference. |

A stable release represents completion of a defined project scope, not necessarily the end of future development.

Versioning and release requirements are maintained at the individual repository level.
